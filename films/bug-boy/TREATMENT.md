# The Boy Who Caught Bugs — treatment

*Working title on screen: none (no title card). File name: `bug-boy`.*
*Style: HD-2D (`styles/hd-2d/STYLE.md`). **160.0 s** (revision 2; was 60 s), 1080 × 1920 portrait, 30 fps, English narration (ElevenLabs "Tyler – Natural American Narrator"), burned-in subtitles + `.srt`.*

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A. One tabletop that changes (a single journey).** One satoyama diorama is the stage for the whole childhood: the boy walks it, the camera rises, and the same set is rebuilt in front of us into a suburb. A power line carries the camera indoors. Strength: the urbanisation beat happens *to a place we already love*, not by cutting to a new place. Weakness: alone, it has no full-circle ending.

**B. Chapters of a save file (RPG grammar).** Chapter cards "I. Dr. Bug", "II. The Cable", "III. Game Freak", "IV. 1996". Clear and very "game", but chapter cards eat 4 s each in a 60 s film and the user asked for no title cards. Rejected.

**C. The loop (bookend).** Open on a beetle on a leaf; end on the same leaf after diving out of a handheld screen. Strength: the "it all came from one tiny thing" idea is felt, not stated. Weakness: on its own, the middle is a list.

**Chosen: A inside C.** The film is a loop that begins and ends on one leaf; inside the loop the childhood is one continuous tabletop that transforms, and every later place is entered *through a line or a frame* (wire → cable → tunnel → screen). Each transition is a camera move along a physical object, so the whole film reads as one imagined camera path from a leaf to a world and back.

## 2. Logline and arc

A boy who catches beetles in the fields outside Tokyo watches the fields vanish — and years later, looking at a cable between two handhelds, finds a way to give every child a field of their own.

- **Setup** (0–16 s): the summer countryside; the boy, his net, his notebook, "Dr. Bug".
- **Turn** (16–39 s): the fields become a suburb; years later, a link cable; the idea arrives as a creature crossing the wire.
- **Ending** (39–60 s): years of team work; 1996, the screen lights; the world shrinks back to the leaf, and the beetle flies.

## 3. Benchmarks (grammar only)

- **Pixar's *Up*, "Married Life" sequence** — learn: a life compressed into a wordless montage that rides one musical motif, which returns changed at the end. Don't take: its characters, music or shots.
- **Studio Ghibli's summer countryside (*My Neighbor Totoro*)** — learn: satoyama light (white-gold morning haze, deep green shade, cicada wall), the scale of a child against tall trees. Don't take: any character, creature, building or tune.
- The HD-2D games the style names — only the sprite-in-diorama grammar the `STYLE.md` already describes.

## 4. Rights and accuracy rules for this film

- The words "Pokémon", "Game Boy", "Game Freak", "Pokémon Red & Green" are spoken and captioned as historical facts. **No logos, no Pokémon characters, no official music or sounds, no Nintendo hardware branding** are drawn. The handhelds are generic grey "brick" handhelds with a green screen and no lettering. All creatures are original designs (glow-beetle, lantern moth, puff-seed, pebble snail) and must not resemble any Pokémon.
- The boy and the young man are generic sprites, not a likeness. No invented dialogue. The beetle is a symbol of the hobby, not "the" beetle that caused anything. The cable creature is shown as *imagination* (dream-glow, pixel sparkle, happens inside his look).
- Facts and sources: `FACTS.md`.

## Production notes

- 160 s × 30 fps = 4,800 frames at ≈ 2.5–3 s each in this cloud machine (software WebGL): about 2–3 h of rendering for the final, plus previews. Worth it, but it means fewer full re-renders.
- The score grows from 6 to 8 sections: Childhood → Urbanisation → Discovery → Development → Launch → **Numbers (bouncy, staccato, building)** → **Auction (playful tension, pizzicato + timpani hits, a comic "stinger")** → **Future/Ending (motif in full, then the quiet leaf chord)**.
