# THE MISSING PAGE — treatment

Golden Hour Stickman (the user's own style, `my-styles/golden-hour-stickman/STYLE.md`) · 1080×1920 · 30 fps · about 92 s · English · no narrator.

## Brief (from the user, complete)

- The user's screenplay is the script: three voices (Examiner, Arrogant Candidate "the Candidate", Second Candidate), ElevenLabs voices, no narrator.
- Cast colours (one bold garment colour per lead): Candidate deep-blue suit · Examiner charcoal suit with a dark red tie · Second Candidate olive-brown suit. Extras are quieter suits tinted toward the haze.
- Music: an original, licensed piano-and-strings score for the warm part; Rachmaninoff, Prelude in C-sharp minor, Op. 3 No. 2 (public-domain composition) for the reveal, as a public-domain/CC0 recording or a new rendering on a real sampled grand.
- No storyboard stop, no final caption, cut to black at the peak.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **Straight chronology, one planted clue** (chosen). Hall → exam → victory walk → elevator → collapse, exactly the order the screenplay gives. The one change I make is where the clue sits: in the exam montage the Second Candidate turns his sheet over, *out of focus in the background*, while the Candidate writes in the foreground (the style's "background hand"). Nobody reads it the first time; on a second viewing it's there. The audience still learns about the back of the page at the same moment as the Candidate.
2. **Cold open on the frozen face, then "two hours earlier".** A strong hook, but it gives the punchline away and turns 70 seconds of confidence into dramatic irony. Rejected: the screenplay says the audience must find out with him.
3. **Expectation / reality split screen.** The Candidate's imagined promotion on top, the real exam below. Too clever for the joke: the comedy needs us to believe him, not doubt him. Rejected.

Why 1: the joke is a pure reveal, so the structure must hide it. Comedy comes from escalating confidence (each beat makes him surer: the salute, finishing first, the handshake), and the horror ending works because the first 70 seconds are played sincerely.

- **Opening image:** the quiet hall in golden morning light, a clock ticking at 8:59, a tie being adjusted. Then the door bangs open on the 2nd second (the hook).
- **Ending:** the reverse shot in the elevator. The Candidate is flattened against the closed steel doors, his hollow face reflected in the brushed metal, the Second Candidate turned toward him with one eyebrow raised, the floor indicator ticking 7 → 6. Low piano impact, then black.
- **Score shape:** quiet → elegant and rhythmic → ridiculously triumphant → calm → **silence** → Rachmaninoff's three bell octaves → black.

## 2. Logline and arc

A cocky candidate finishes a career-deciding exam first, shakes the examiner's hand like a champion, and finds out in the elevator that the paper had a back.

- **Setup:** 80 percent or nothing. Everyone is terrified except him ("See you at the top, chief!").
- **Build:** he breezes through, finishes first, hands in, signs, handshake, exits like a hero.
- **Turn:** "Those technical questions on the back were brutal." "…What other page?"
- **Ending:** psychological horror over a missing page. The doors close on his escape.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Mockumentary sitcom reaction shots.** Learn: hold on the face one beat longer than comfortable; the joke is the face. Don't take: talking heads, characters, locations.
- **Psychological-thriller realisation beats** (the push-in on a frozen face, sound dropping out to a heartbeat, the cold grade). Learn: sound subtraction before the hit, slow push, one memory flash. Don't take: specific shots, music cues, the dolly-zoom cliché used for a whole scene.
- **Silent-comedy timing for the escape.** Learn: one clean action and one sound for the door, then the cut. Don't take: gags or characters.

## 4. Light and geography

- **Exam hall:** floor-to-ceiling windows on screen left; low golden morning sun from the left (light direction −0.8, 0.5 for every figure); long pane patches on the floor that **move across the room during the time-lapse** (light tells time). The examiner's raised desk and the door are on screen right. A wall screen reads EXECUTIVE QUALIFICATION ASSESSMENT.
- **Hallway:** one-point perspective, warm ceiling lights, polished floor reflections, glass partitions, the elevator at the far end.
- **Elevator:** brushed metal, warm ceiling panel, handrail on the back wall. The **doors are in front of the men** (people face the doors), so:
  - the front shot (camera where the doors are) shows both faces; the doors' position reads as **a strip of hallway light across the men that narrows as the doors close**, and widens when they open;
  - the reverse shot (camera at the back wall) shows the doors, the floor indicator above them and the backs of both men.
  The elevator leaves the exam floor (12) with the doors closed, then stops at floor 7 during the Candidate's frozen horror: the doors open on a cold, empty floor (his way out) and start closing while he stares. When he snaps out of it they are almost shut. He lunges, they close, DING, 7 → 6, still going down. This keeps the doors physically honest: nothing passes through anyone.

## 5. Exam-page logic (the plot)

- One double-sided sheet. Front: **PART A — STATISTICS · 50 POINTS**. Back: **PART B — TECHNICAL ASSESSMENT · 50 POINTS**. Header on the front: EXECUTIVE QUALIFICATION ASSESSMENT · TOTAL 100 · PASS MARK 80.
- During the opening, only over-the-shoulder views of the front: the word STATISTICS reads, the small print doesn't. No TURN OVER.
- The Candidate's hand only ever writes on the front; he never lifts the sheet. The Second Candidate flips his sheet (background, soft focus) and writes on both sides.
- Hand-in: the Candidate puts his one sheet on the examiner's pile, then signs a separate **HAND-IN REGISTER** (a clipboard with lined rows). The Second Candidate later signs the next row of the same register.
- Memory flash: the sheet flips over in white light. The back is blank under PART B — TECHNICAL ASSESSMENT · 50 POINTS. Then a hard insert of the header's PASS MARK 80. 50 ≤ 100 − 50 < 80: the maths is on screen in the page's own print, with no added text.
