HOW DOES SPERM VOLUME ACTUALLY INCREASE?
SOURCES & ACCURACY NOTES (not for the spoken script)
==================================================================
Research was done with web search on 2026-11-09. The entries below are
sources that the searches surfaced and whose content was summarized during
research. Primary papers were not all read in full. Check the full text
before quoting exact figures in a pinned comment or description.

ANATOMY & COMPOSITION
- NCI SEER Training Modules, "Accessory Glands" (male reproductive system).
  https://training.seer.cancer.gov/anatomy/reproductive/male/glands.html
  Seminal vesicles about 60% of volume; ejaculate about 1.5-6 mL.
- Encyclopaedia Britannica, "Semen". https://www.britannica.com/topic/semen
  Seminal vesicles largest share, prostate about 30%, volume about 2-5 mL.
- PMC review schematic of ejaculate composition (PMC2203235): seminal
  vesicles 50-80%, prostate 20-40%.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2203235/

REFERENCE VALUES
- WHO Laboratory Manual for the Examination and Processing of Human Semen,
  6th ed. (2021). Semen volume lower reference limit 1.4 mL (5th centile).
  As reported by Manchester University NHS FT andrology:
  https://mft.nhs.uk/app/uploads/sites/4/2023/01/Lower-reference-limits-2021.pdf

ABSTINENCE
- Elzanaty S. et al., "Influence of the abstinence period on human sperm
  quality", 2004. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15236990/
- Systematic review of abstinence and semen parameters (28 publications;
  15 of 17 found significant volume increases), PMC5364080.
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5364080

AGE
- Eskenazi B. et al. 2003 (healthy men aged 22-80; volume about -0.03 mL
  per year). https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/802878

  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00404-024-07448-8

CAVEATS & HONEST FRAMING
------------------------------------------------------------------
1. "Sperm volume" vs semen volume. The title uses the search phrase; the
   film itself corrects it. Keep that correction. It is the hook.
2. Percentages are ranges across sources. The script uses "about two to
   five percent" and "roughly two thirds" on purpose.
3. Abstinence. The direction is well supported, but no per-day rate is
   claimed. Very long abstinence lowers motility (and can affect DNA
   quality in some studies), so the "sweet spot" is a trade-off.
4. Supplements:
   - Zinc: no benefit in the large FAZST trial in men in general. The
     deficiency nuance is plausible but less directly proven, hence
     "probably".
   - CoQ10: the most consistent signal is motility. No meaningful volume
     effect. Hence "may help sperm swim".
   - Ashwagandha: one small pilot trial, one branded extract, men with low
     sperm counts. Not replicated at scale. Safety: rare cases of
     drug-induced liver injury have been reported with ashwagandha, and it
     can interact with thyroid, sedative, immunosuppressant and diabetes
     medications. It is generally advised against for people with thyroid
     or autoimmune conditions. (Background knowledge, not verified in this
     research pass; confirm before stating in a description.)
   - Also studied, not in the narration: L-carnitine (motility) and
     vitamin D (if deficient). Evidence is mixed to modest.
   - None of these is proven to "super-size" volume in healthy men.
5. Testosterone. Suppression is strong and common (azoospermia in about
   65% in one series) and usually reversible, though recovery can take
   months. Over-the-counter "testosterone boosters" are a different thing
   (mostly herbal blends) with poor evidence. The film refers to actual
   testosterone.
6. Not medical advice. A sudden drop in volume, pain, blood in semen,
   or trying to conceive for 12 months without success are reasons to see
   a doctor or urologist.
7. Not claimed: hydration effects, the order of fractions within an
   ejaculate, "load size" and fertility links, or any per-pill percentage.
8. Visual simplifications: sagittal schematic, not to scale; the cylinder
   layers are a compositional chart, not real sedimentation; the "sluggish
   sperm yawn" and "sugar fuels sperm wiggle" are symbolic.
