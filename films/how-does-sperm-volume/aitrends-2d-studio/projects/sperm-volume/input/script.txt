Every ad online promises more sperm volume. Here's the twist... it's barely sperm. Sperm make up only about two to five percent of the total. So how does that volume actually go up? First, find the factories. Sperm are made in the testes, then stored in a coiled tube, the epididymis. But most of the fluid comes from the seminal vesicles, tucked behind the bladder. They supply roughly two thirds of it, packed with fructose, a sugar that fuels sperm. The prostate adds most of the rest: a thin, zinc-rich fluid full of enzymes. At release, the streams merge. A muscle ring seals the bladder. If it fails, semen flows backward. So volume is mostly glands refilling. The biggest lever isn't a pill. It's time. Skip a few days and volume climbs. Wait too long, and sperm get sluggish. Age, low testosterone, and prostate drugs like dutasteride can all shrink it. But taking testosterone to fix it backfires. Your brain sees plenty and shuts sperm production down. Supplements? Zinc probably only helps if you're low. CoQ10 may help sperm swim. Give it three months. Ashwagandha raised volume in one small trial. A sudden drop? Talk to a doctor. So sperm volume is really gland volume. Time, hormones, plumbing... and almost no sperm.
