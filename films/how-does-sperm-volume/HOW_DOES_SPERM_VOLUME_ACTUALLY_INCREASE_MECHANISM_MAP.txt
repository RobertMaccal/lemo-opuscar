HOW DOES SPERM VOLUME ACTUALLY INCREASE?
MECHANISM MAP + ACCURACY LEDGER
==================================================================

TERMINOLOGY NOTE
"Sperm volume" is the everyday phrase people search for. Strictly it means
semen (ejaculate) volume. The film keeps the popular phrase in the title and
hook, then makes the correction its central reveal: sperm cells are only
about 2-5% of the volume. The narration says "semen" once it has made that
correction.

------------------------------------------------------------------
CAUSAL SPINE (input -> components -> transformation -> output -> control)
------------------------------------------------------------------

1. PRODUCTION (input). The testes make sperm continuously. One full cycle of
   sperm development plus transit takes roughly 2.5-3 months, so any change
   that affects sperm production shows up months later, not days later.

2. STORAGE. Sperm mature and are stored in the epididymis, a tightly coiled
   tube on the back of each testis. The vas deferens carries them up and
   behind the bladder to the ejaculatory ducts.

3. THE MAIN SOURCE OF THE FLUID. The two seminal vesicles, behind the bladder,
   make roughly 50-80% of the volume (most sources: about 60-70%). Their fluid
   is rich in fructose (an energy source for sperm), plus proteins and
   prostaglandins. Their secretion depends on androgens (testosterone).

4. SECOND SOURCE. The prostate, below the bladder and wrapped around the
   urethra, adds about 20-30%: a thin fluid with citrate, zinc and enzymes
   (for example PSA, which later liquefies the semen).
   Bulbourethral glands add a small amount (under 1-5%).

5. EMISSION (merge). During ejaculation, sympathetic nerves contract the
   smooth muscle of the vas deferens, seminal vesicles and prostate. The
   streams meet in the prostatic urethra. At the same time the bladder neck
   (a ring of muscle) closes.

6. EXPULSION. A reflex through the pudendal nerve drives rhythmic
   contractions of the pelvic-floor muscles (bulbospongiosus), pushing semen
   out through the urethra.

7. FAILURE MODE / FEEDBACK. If the bladder neck doesn't close, semen goes
   backward into the bladder (retrograde ejaculation), so less or nothing
   comes out. Recognized causes include diabetes, prostate or pelvic surgery,
   spinal injury and some medications.

8. REFILL (control). The glands store their secretions and refill between
   ejaculations. Longer gaps between ejaculations raise volume (and count);
   very long gaps tend to lower motility. The WHO lab protocol uses 2-7 days
   of abstinence.

9. HORMONE LOOP (counterintuitive). The brain (hypothalamus and pituitary)
   releases LH and FSH, which tell the testes to make testosterone and sperm.
   Taking testosterone from outside makes the brain sense there is plenty, so
   it cuts LH and FSH. Testosterone inside the testes falls, and sperm
   production collapses, often to near zero. It usually recovers after
   stopping, but that can take months.

10. PRACTICAL CONSEQUENCE. Semen volume is mostly gland output. The levers
    are time between ejaculations, healthy androgen levels, intact ducts and
    bladder-neck function, age, and medications. It is not sperm count.
    Supplements mainly target sperm quality, with modest or uncertain
    evidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------
ACCURACY LEDGER
claim | evidence | confidence | simplification risk | required visual
------------------------------------------------------------------

L1  Sperm cells are about 2-5% of ejaculate volume.
    Evidence: NCI SEER training module; Britannica; vasectomy literature
    (vasal contribution under 10%). Confidence: HIGH (sources vary 1-5%).
    Risk: the exact percentage varies by source, so say "about two to five
    percent". Visual: measuring cylinder with a thin cream layer.

L2  Seminal vesicles supply roughly two thirds of the fluid.
    Evidence: SEER (about 60%), Britannica, PMC review schematic (50-80%).
    Confidence: HIGH that they are the largest share, MODERATE on the exact
    fraction. Risk: "two thirds" is a rounded midpoint. Visual: amber layer,
    about 65%.

L3  Seminal vesicle fluid contains fructose that fuels sperm.
    Evidence: standard physiology texts; SEER. Confidence: HIGH.

L4  The prostate adds most of the remainder: a thin fluid with zinc and
    enzymes. Evidence: SEER, Britannica (about 20-30%). Confidence: HIGH.
    Visual: teal layer, about 30%.

L5  Sperm are made in the testes and stored or matured in the epididymis.
    Confidence: HIGH (textbook anatomy).

L6  During emission the bladder neck closes. Failure gives retrograde
    ejaculation. Evidence: PMC ejaculation-physiology review; GPnotebook.
    Confidence: HIGH. Visual: muscle ring closing (cause), then a coral
    backward arrow in the "fail" inset (effect).

L7  Longer abstinence raises volume. Very long abstinence lowers motility.
    Evidence: systematic review of 28 publications (15 of 17 volume studies
    found significant increases); Elzanaty et al. 2004 (PubMed 15236990);
    2026 observational data (about 1.8 mL at 1 day vs about 3.5 mL at 6 or
    more days). Confidence: HIGH for direction, LOW for any per-day rate.
    Risk: individual variation. No per-day number is spoken. Visual: calendar
    pages flip while the gland reservoirs refill.

L8  Volume tends to decline with age. Evidence: Eskenazi et al. 2003 (about
    0.03 mL per year); Malaysian cohort of 1,775 men. Confidence: MODERATE.
    One large 2024 Italian study found no age correlation. Phrase it as "can"
    and keep it brief.

L9  Low testosterone reduces gland secretion, so it lowers volume.
    Evidence: androgen dependence of the accessory glands (standard
    physiology; patient-education sources). Confidence: MODERATE-HIGH.

L10 5-alpha-reductase inhibitors (dutasteride; less so finasteride) reduce
    semen volume. Evidence: Amory et al., J Clin Endocrinol Metab 2007 RCT
    (PubMed 17299062); Harvard Health summary. Confidence: HIGH for
    dutasteride. Risk: magnitudes differ between reports (about 20-30%), so
    no number is spoken.

L11 Taking external testosterone suppresses LH/FSH and sperm production.
    Evidence: reviews (PMC4708215) summarising AUA and Endocrine Society
    advice against testosterone for men wanting fertility within 6-12 months;
    about 65% of men became azoospermic in one contraceptive series.
    Confidence: HIGH. Risk: "shuts down" simplifies a strong suppression that
    is usually reversible. Note it on screen and in the caption. Visual:
    brain-to-testes signal line dims as a testosterone syringe or gel icon
    floods the bloodstream.

L12 Zinc plus folic acid did not improve semen quality or live births in men
    in general. Evidence: FAZST RCT, JAMA 2020 (n=2,370, PubMed 31910279).
    Confidence: HIGH. The idea that zinc helps if you are deficient is
    biologically plausible but less directly proven, so the spoken line says
    "probably".

L13 CoQ10 may improve sperm motility, with no clear effect on volume.
    Evidence: Lafuente et al. 2013 meta-analysis (PubMed 23912751); 2020
    pooled analysis (PubMed 32271472): "profound" on motility, "meagre" on
    volume and count. 2025 Frontiers meta-analysis: no significant effect on
    volume. Confidence: MODERATE. The film claims only "may help sperm swim".

L14 Ashwagandha raised semen volume in one small trial.
    Evidence: Ambiye et al. 2013 pilot RCT, 46 oligospermic men, KSM-66 extract
    675 mg/day for 90 days: volume 1.74 to 2.76 mL, concentration up 167% vs
    placebo (PMC3863556). Confidence: LOW-MODERATE (small, single extract,
    men with low counts, not replicated at scale). Visual must say "1 SMALL
    STUDY". Safety: rare liver-injury reports and interactions; not for
    everyone (see SOURCES caveats).

L15 Broad antioxidant use for male subfertility: very-low-certainty evidence
    of benefit. Evidence: Cochrane review CD007411.pub5 (2022). Used for
    context in notes, not spoken.

L16 New sperm take about 3 months, so supplement trials run about 90 days.
    Evidence: spermatogenesis about 64-74 days plus epididymal transit; the
    Ambiye trial lasted 90 days. Confidence: HIGH (rounded).

L17 Typical ejaculate volume is about 1.5-5 mL. The WHO 2021 lower reference
    limit is 1.4 mL (5th centile, fertile men), not a fertility cutoff.
    Evidence: WHO Laboratory Manual 6th ed. (reported by NHS MFT andrology);
    SEER. Confidence: HIGH. On-screen only.

L18 Vasectomy doesn't significantly change semen volume. This supports L1.
    Evidence: Naelitz et al., Andrology 2025 case-control. Confidence: HIGH.
    Optional on-screen easter egg in clip 2, not spoken.

------------------------------------------------------------------
INTENTIONAL SIMPLIFICATIONS (flag in visuals and notes)
------------------------------------------------------------------
- Anatomy is drawn as a clinical, non-explicit sagittal schematic. Organ
  sizes and positions are relative, not to scale.
- "Muscle ring" means the internal urethral sphincter at the bladder neck.
- "Shuts sperm production down" means strong suppression, often to near
  zero, usually reversible after stopping.
- Zinc and the deficiency nuance: plausible, less directly proven.
- The order in which each gland's fluid comes out is NOT shown or claimed.
  It was not verified in this research pass.
- Hydration is not claimed. Semen is mostly water, but no good evidence was
  found that drinking extra water increases volume in hydrated men.
