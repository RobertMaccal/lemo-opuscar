# Hung Out to Dry: treatment

A classic dark-comedy joke told as if it were a true, moving documentary story. The narrator (Tyler) plays it completely straight: a heartfelt score, sincere pacing, a hero. Only the final line reveals the twist. The picture then shows exactly what he meant: the rescued patient is dead, pegged up with the laundry on the balcony's washing line, limp and dripping, with X'd-out cartoon eyes. That's dark humour: Walter saved him, then 'hung him up to dry'.

## Three structures

1. **Cold open on the punchline, then rewind.** This kills the joke.
2. **Interview-style mock documentary, with the doctor recalling it in flashback.** That needs a second voice and runs longer than a minute.
3. **Straight chronological telling with the twist only in the last line (chosen).** The setup earns empathy: a fall, a heroic rescue, the doctor moved. The office scene builds to a warm discharge. Then comes the doctor's last question, a deadpan answer, silence, and the reveal.

## Comedy rules

- Take it seriously until the last four words. Use warm daylight, an emotional piano and strings, and sincere faces.
- The punchline gets silence, then the picture: we pull back from the doctor's frozen face to the balcony.
- Harold is dead in the cartoon way: limp, pale, X eyes, tongue out, a fly buzzing round, hanging by clothespins on his pyjama shoulders next to the towels and socks. There is no noose. It's literally laundry, which is the joke, and it keeps the post safe on Instagram.
- End on a silent button: the doctor quietly takes the discharge paper back. A comic pizzicato sting plays.

## Look

The same style as *The Footprints* and *The Brides in the Bath*:
- 2.5D toon sets with ink outlines
- flat round-head characters
- constant camera motion with depth of field
- dissolves, and one-word captions

The hospital is a sunlit 1960s institution:
- pale mint and cream walls
- a turquoise pool with white tiles
- clipped hedges and deck chairs
- one balcony with a washing line above the pool

## Framing rules (carried over)

- Every prop the story needs is fully in frame.
- Characters stand on surfaces and have contact shadows.
- Heads are never cropped.
- The framing check (`qc.mjs`) runs on every shot.
