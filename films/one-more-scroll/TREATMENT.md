# ONE MORE SCROLL — Episode 1 · Treatment

1920×1080 · 24 fps · 4:25 · English · dialogue only, no narrator.
Look: **aiTrends 2D** (the user's aiTrends 2D Studio): black stick figures with rim light, one signature colour per character, low-key flat-shaded 2.5D sets, giant face close-ups, a freeze-frame, low-poly 3D shots; grade "midnight" (cold navy, blue-white rims, sodium practicals). Every frame drawn in code (canvas + three.js), rendered frame by frame.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **Straight line, one day + one night (as briefed).** Space → bed → scroll → alarm → bus → office → boss → lunch → second night → stranger. Linear clarity; risk: the middle (office) reads as a list.
2. **The clock as spine.** Every scene opens or closes on a clock face (bedside clock, phone lock screen, bus clock, office wall clock, microwave). Each clock is a match cut into the next. The audience starts *counting* Jeff's sleep with us: 1 h 30 m, then 0.
3. **The phone as a portal.** Every transition passes through the Instickram screen: we fall into videos, the screen glow becomes the bathroom light, the bus window reflection becomes a feed, the boss's face becomes a frozen video tile.

**Chosen: 1 with 2 woven through, and 3 used only where it counts.** The storyline is fixed by the brief and must stay legible, so the line stays straight. The clock spine gives the time-lapses a single visual language (every jump in time is the same clock gesture, sped up) and makes the cost of sleep countable. The phone portal is spent three times: into the videos (scene 2), the montage (scene 8), and the stranger's face lit like a phone screen (scene 9). Using it everywhere would blunt it.

## 2. Logline and arc

A tired office worker keeps trading "just a few more minutes" of sleep for an endless feed, until the version of himself who already paid the price walks into his bedroom.

- **Setup (0:00–1:05):** comfort. A beautiful world at night, a warm bed, funny videos. We like Jeff and laugh with him.
- **Turn (1:05–3:15):** cost. 4:30 AM, the cold morning, a commute where his head keeps dropping, an office where he is half there, a boss who threatens his job and gets "whatever".
- **Ending (3:15–4:05):** recognition, then dread. He goes straight back to the phone. Scrolling, alarms ignored, then the knock. "I am you. And you're in trouble." Black.

## 3. Benchmarks (grammar only)

- **Psychological-thriller title sequences and endings** (slow push-ins, held silences, one light source): learn the restraint of the final 20 seconds: no music, a single hum, the cut on a breath. Don't take shots, music or typography.
- **Contemporary 2D shorts lit like live action** (window light, phone-glow faces, rim light, layered blur): learn how motivated light and a few well-placed sources make simple characters feel cinematic. Don't take designs or palettes.
- **Workplace sitcoms**: learn the held deadpan reaction against a loud partner (the boss scene). Don't take staging or characters.

## 9. Subtitles and titles

- No burned-in subtitles (the brief asks for a cinematic film; dialogue is clear). A complete `.srt` is delivered.
- Title card at the very start? No: the opening is uninterrupted. "ONE MORE SCROLL" appears once, small and late, as the Instickram loading screen's tagline? No; the title appears over the black after scene 2's phone goes dark ("ONE MORE SCROLL", 3 s, in the silence before the alarm) — the title arrives on the first lie.
- End card: "TO BE CONTINUED…" in a clean condensed sans (Fredoka-based lockup), cold white on black, slight flicker of phone glow.
- In-world text (clocks, app UI, spreadsheet) is drawn crisp; fonts OFL, self-hosted.
