# CPR CLASS GONE WRONG — treatment

Golden Hour Stickman (the user's own style, `my-styles/golden-hour-stickman/STYLE.md`).
1080×1920, 30 fps, target 76–78 s (hard ceiling 80 s). English, ElevenLabs voices, no narrator.
**No music of any kind** (the user's brief overrides the style's piano score): the only rhythm comes from
the characters' own voices, chest compressions, foot taps and claps.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **One room, three waves of wrong** (chosen). A single unbroken classroom period in real time; every
   wave of chaos is bigger than the last (one wrong song → one irrelevant remark → a whole room singing),
   the teacher shuts it down, and when the audience thinks the joke is spent, two quiet students make it
   darker. A sitcom "runner" structure: the mannequin is the straight man who never moves.
2. **Expectation / reality split.** A clean "how CPR should go" panel (Call 911 → AED → 110/min) runs
   beside the class's version and gets further out of sync. Clear, but turns the film into a lesson and
   halves the faces in a vertical frame. Rejected: the faces are the comedy.
3. **The assessment sheet.** The teacher's clipboard checklist ("Checked response ☐ … Called 911 ☐") is
   the spine; each beat fails a box. Neat device, but it telegraphs the jokes and adds text to read.
   Kept only as one prop: the teacher's verdict lists the three failures in that order.

**Why 1:** the brief's comedy is contagion and escalation, which needs continuous space and time. One
room, one light direction, a recurring wide shot to re-anchor the geography each time things escalate.

- **Opening image:** the calm before — a quiet front-of-class wide in golden light, dust drifting over
  the mannequin, the teacher's "All right, class." The film's title sits on a paper label for 3 s;
  a small grey identifier "FICTIONAL FIRST-AID CLASSROOM COMEDY" sits above it.
- **Ending:** a static medium shot for the donor-card line, then a silent freeze montage (pen drops,
  Christopher's eyes, the teacher's slow turn), one half-second of nothing, hard cut to black.
  No outro, no laugh track, no explanation.
- **Score shape:** none. The "score" is the 110 BPM chant that the class builds out of the CPR rhythm
  itself — the compressions are the metronome — and its abrupt death on "STOP!".

## 2. Logline and arc

A confident student volunteers to demo CPR, sings the wrong song, gets corrected, and turns the
corrected rhythm into a class-wide a cappella disco that makes him forget the patient entirely —
and the post-mortem discussion only gets worse.

- **Setup (0–22 s):** ordinary lesson; volunteer is sure of himself; wrong song; correction.
- **Turn (22–49 s):** random interruption, then the rhythm spreads 1 → 2 → 4 → everyone; he stands
  up and dances. The central error: he stops compressions.
- **Ending (49–78 s):** "STOP!"; the deadpan verdict "You lost him."; "What do we do next?";
  "bury him?"; the donor-card line said like common knowledge; frozen disbelief; black.

## 3. Benchmark

Ensemble classroom/workplace sitcoms with a single-camera, mockumentary rhythm.
- **Learn:** the held reaction shot; the straight man who reacts with a blink rather than a shout;
  re-establishing wides when the room escalates; letting a silence sit for a full beat before cutting.
- **Don't take:** any characters, settings, shots, catchphrases, theme music, or the Bee Gees /
  Gloria Gaynor songs. No recognisable melody is reconstructed; the chant is original words and rhythm.

## 7. Cue map (rhythm grid)

- No music. **110 BPM** grid (beat 0.545 s, bar 2.18 s) from the first resumed compression (shot 12) to "STOP".
- Compressions land on every beat while he is kneeling (110/min is inside the 100–120 range).
- Chant bars: 1–2 volunteer alone ("ah, ah, ah, ah" / "keep him alive"); 3 + Maya humming;
  4 + Lena; 5 four voices ("push, push, keep him alive!" + "ooh-ooh"); 6 everyone, foot taps and claps;
  last compression on bar 6 beat 1; stands bars 6–7; full disco point on bar 8 beat 1. "STOP" cuts it mid-bar.
- Each voice enters with its own ±20–50 ms looseness, its own pitch and pan; no perfect unison.
