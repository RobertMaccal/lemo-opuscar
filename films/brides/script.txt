[serious] In 1915, a detective grabbed a woman in a bathtub by her feet… and pulled her under. On purpose. Within seconds, she stopped moving. And that's how he caught a killer.

It started with a newspaper. A father read about a young bride in London, found dead in her bath. His own daughter, Alice, had died the exact same way. A new bride. One year earlier.

Her husband was George Smith. The London bride's husband was John Lloyd. Different names… same story.

Detective Inspector Arthur Neil started digging, and he found a third. Bessie. 1912. Drowned in her bath, five days after making a will.

Three brides. Three bathtubs. One man, with a different name every time. And every time, the money went to him.

But there was a problem. No bruises. No sign of a struggle. Bessie was five foot seven. Her bath was only five feet long. So how do you drown a grown woman… without leaving a single mark?

Neil had the bathtubs brought to London… and hired a trained diver. She sat in the tub. Then, without warning, Neil grabbed her feet… and pulled. Her head slid under. Water rushed into her nose and throat. And she went limp. It took over half an hour to bring her back.

That was the trick. No struggle. No marks. Just one sudden pull.

The jury needed twenty minutes. George Joseph Smith was hanged that August. But three women died before anyone noticed. If one father hadn't read one newspaper… how many more brides would there have been?
