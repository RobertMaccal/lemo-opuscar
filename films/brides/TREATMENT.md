# The Brides in the Bath: treatment

**Case:** George Joseph Smith, England 1912–1915. He killed three wives in their baths: Bessie Mundy (Herne Bay, 1912), Alice Burnham (Blackpool, 1913) and Margaret Lofty (Highgate, London, 1914). Each death was ruled an accident.
- **How it came apart:** Alice's father read a News of the World article about the London bride, and a letter from Blackpool reached the police.
- **The solution:** Detective Inspector Arthur Neil and the pathologist Bernard Spilsbury had the actual tubs brought to London. Neil pulled a trained diver's feet without warning; she went limp and needed over half an hour to revive.
- **The verdict:** the jury took about 20 minutes. Smith was hanged at Maidstone on 13 August 1915.

## Three structures

1. **Chronological.** Smith meets Bessie and the deaths follow in order, then the investigation and the trial. It's honest, but the out-of-the-box solution arrives at minute one. The hook is weak: it opens on a wedding.
2. **The newspaper first.** It opens on the father reading the article: "Your daughter died in a bath. So did hers." That's a good mystery, but the first five seconds are a man reading, which is not strong enough to stop a scroll.
3. **The experiment first, then explained (chosen).** The first five seconds give away the most shocking image of the case: a detective pulling a woman under the water on purpose, and she stops moving. The viewer doesn't know why. The film then rewinds to the newspaper, finds three brides and three tubs, and sets up the impossible puzzle: no marks, and a bath shorter than the woman. It returns to the experiment as the payoff, so the hook becomes the solution. It ends on a question that leaves people thinking.

## Look

The same style as *The Footprints*:
- 2.5D toon sets with thick ink outlines
- flat round-head characters
- constant camera motion with depth of field
- dissolves, and one-word captions at 70% of the frame height

**Palette:** cold Edwardian teal and green tiles for the bathrooms, sickly gas-lamp amber, pale porcelain, sepia newsprint, and one red accent for the keywords.

**What happens on screen:** death is only implied. We see an empty tub, still water, a hand on the rim and a doctor's bag. The experiment is shown honestly: the diver is in a bathing costume and comes up alive.

## Rules for this film (from the user)

- **Frame every object fully.** Every prop the narration names (newspaper, will, tub, photograph) sits wholly inside the frame with a margin. There are no extreme close-ups that crop it.
- **Ground every character.** Feet stand on a floor and get a contact shadow. Seated characters sit on something. No one floats.
- **Keep the world complete.** Sets extend past every camera view, so there are no voids or edges.
- **Leave the voice untouched.** Natural speed and no whispers. Only long silences are trimmed.
