# THE TOILET DREAM — THE ULTIMATE BETRAYAL · Treatment

70 s · 1080×1920 vertical · 30 fps · no dialogue, no narration, no on-screen text · original piano score + foley.

## Style

**Golden Hour Stickman 暖光火柴人**, the owner's own saved style (`my-styles/golden-hour-stickman/STYLE.md`), confirmed by the owner from its style frame and acting sheet. Its engine (`kit.js`, `rig.js`, the rim-light compositor and the post stack) is copied into `src/` and extended only with what this film needs: bare feet, a pyjama shirt, bed-head hair, a bed with a blanket that covers, lifts, and can be thrown or soaked.

Night version of the style's colour logic: the warm ground comes from practicals (the bedside lamp and the bathroom ceiling light), and the cool counterweight is lavender moonlight. Dusty lavender and indigo are the shadows; the pyjama blue is the most saturated thing in every frame.

Departures from the style's defaults, all requested in the brief: no voices, no captions, no title card, and an **original** piano score instead of a public-domain recording.

## Three candidate structures

1. **Four loops, same shots each time (chosen).** One real sleeping shot, then four trips built from the *same* set-ups (bed → hallway → bathroom → close-up). The joke is in the repetition. Each loop is shorter and harder, then the reality-check loop slows down, and the real bed comes last. It suits a vertical short: the audience learns the pattern in 20 s and then waits for it to break.
2. **Clock-driven.** A bedside clock frames every awakening, and the minutes tell us how much real time has passed. It's clever, but the clock becomes the subject, it needs text-like reading at phone size, and it gives the dream away.
3. **Reverse reveal.** Open on the wet bed, then rewind through the dreams. That kills the final punchline, which the brief rules out.

Choice: **1**. The opening image is the real bed, dry and calm. The ending is the same bed from the same angle, wet. The score shape is one waltz theme that keeps being cut off on the downbeat where it should resolve. The last time it never resolves at all.

## Logline and arc

A sleepy man needs to pee. Three times he reaches the toilet and wakes up. On the fourth try he proves to himself that he's awake, finally goes, and we cut to his bed.

Setup (0–9: the trip looks normal) → escalation (9–40: wake, check, repeat, faster each time) → reassurance (40–49: reality checks) → false triumph (49–62.5) → turn (62.5: the real bed) → hold (to 69) → black.

## Benchmark

- **Learn from:** silent physical comedy shorts and Pixar-style pantomime shorts (the rule of three, the reset gag, the held reaction, silence before a payoff); *Inception*-style match cuts (the same framing across a cut so reality "snaps"); Edward Hopper's night interiors (one warm practical against cold blue windows).
- **Don't take:** any characters, designs, music, shots or gags from those works.

## Hidden tells (for rewatching only)

- The wall clock reads 3:07 in every shot. In the two real shots (opening and finale) its second hand moves; in every "awakening" it is frozen.
- The bathroom towel hangs to the left in dreams 1 and 3 and to the right in dreams 2 and 4.
- In dream 1 he flips the hallway switch to turn the bathroom light on. In every later trip it's already on: nobody ever turned it on in the real flat.
