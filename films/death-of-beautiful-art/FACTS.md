## Interpretations (the film's argument, phrased as such)

- "Once, art mostly meant skill, observation and imagination." A broad generalisation about pre-modern expectations, voiced as "mostly".
- "Van Gogh turned the view … into a sky that moves." Descriptive.
- "What if the artist's idea mattered more than the artist's hand?" Framed as a question, grounded in F13.
- "By the late sixties, art could be an instruction. An ordinary object. A question." Grounded in F14–F16 (LeWitt's wall drawings from 1968 are written instructions; instruction pieces from the early 1960s).
- "Art no longer has to be beautiful to be called art." The film's thesis, voiced as a conclusion, not as a cited fact.

## Opinions (attributed, not asserted)

- Supporters: conceptual art challenges how we think (F19 as the on-screen example).
- Critics: the role of collectors, institutions, publicity and speculation. Voiced as a question. **No critic is quoted on screen unless we find a real, citable quote** (none is invented).
