# TREATMENT — The Death of Beautiful Art?

Style: **Visual Investigation** (`styles/visual-investigation/STYLE.md`) · 1920 × 1080 native, 30 fps · H.264 + AAC · target 2:45 (2:30–3:00) · English voice + burned-in subtitles + `.srt`.

Written before opening the style's `DEMO.md` or demo code (AGENTS.md, "Treatment first").

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **Price cold open → rewind → march forward → return** (your brief). Open on the frozen price, slam the evidence into a split, "rewind" the year readout from 2024 to 1889, then walk forward exhibit by exhibit back to the banana. Strong hook, clear chronology, classic investigative shape.
2. **One question, answered exhibit by exhibit: "What exactly did $6.24 M buy?"** Every section brings one exhibit that answers part of it (skill → choice → idea → certificate → price), each tied by a red link to one zone on an evidence board.
3. **A strict clock, 1889 → 2024.** One year readout runs the whole film; findings drop in where they happen. Very clean, but it would put Duchamp before Guernica and lose the thematic contrast.

**Chosen: 1, with 2's evidence board as its spine.** Structure 1 gives the hook and the ending you asked for. Structure 2 gives the investigation a *finding* and not just a timeline. Every conceptual exhibit carries the same piece of evidence, an **authorship mark**: Duchamp's painted signature "R. MUTT 1917", LeWitt's written instructions, Garau's certificate, Cattelan's signed certificate. Each one sends a red link to one zone, *THE SIGNATURE*, and that zone is what sells for millions. It's the sync point of the film: four independent sources pointing at the same place, the style's core payoff (STYLE §1.1).

## 2. Logline and arc

**Logline.** An investigation desk examines a $6.24 M banana and traces the price back through Van Gogh's sky, Duchamp's signature, Warhol's boxes and an invisible sculpture. It finds that what was really sold was a signed idea, and then asks when the price itself became part of the art.

- **Setup**: disbelief. A banana, a piece of tape, a price.
- **Turn**: "He CHOSE it." (1917). Art moves from *what the artist made* to *what the artist chose and signed*.
- **Escalation**: the idea (1964–67) → the certificate with nothing attached (2021) → the certificate with a replaceable banana attached (2024).
- **Ending**: the opening contrast again, Starry Night facing the banana schematic, the timeline between them. The annotations clear and the final question stays unanswered.

The three questions of your note, as the film's three movements: *"Look what the artist created"* (Seq 2) → *"Look what the artist made us question"* (Seq 3–5) → *"Who decided it was worth millions?"* (Seq 6–8).

## 3. Benchmark (grammar only, never named or copied in the film)

- **Newspaper visual-investigation videos**. *Learn:* one finding per layout, red used only to point, readouts that live inside the picture, source tags. *Don't take:* their branding, music or layouts.
- **Robert Hughes, *The Shock of the New* (BBC, 1980)**, for the voice: a sceptical, dry, unhurried narrator who still loves the paintings. *Learn:* tone, letting a painting hold the screen. *Don't take:* lines or structure.

## 4. Look (inside STYLE.md)

Ground `#0B0C0E`, panels `#141619`, hairlines `#2E3238`, text `#ECEAE4`, secondary `#8B9097`, one accent **`#FF4A2E`** that only points. Sources greyscale with the style's grade and grain. Hard cuts on a 0.5 s grid with 3–4-frame white flashes; only drawn things animate progressively (circles, leaders, highlights, schematic strokes); slow push ≤ 1.06× on photos and paintings.

**⚠ One decision for you: colour on the paintings.** The style is strict: *sources are greyscale; red is the only colour*. Your brief asks for colour in the Van Gogh sequence. My recommendation is **one deliberate exception that becomes the film's idea.** *The Starry Night* appears in full colour in Seq 2 and in the final frame. At the 1917 cut the colour drains out of it in one white flash, and everything after that is greyscale + red. Beauty literally leaves the film at Duchamp, and the last shot puts it back opposite the banana. The alternative is strict greyscale throughout: the brushwork still reads, but the "most beautiful sequence" loses its colour.
