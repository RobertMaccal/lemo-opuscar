# What Assignment? — treatment

15 s · 1080×1920 · 30 fps · English · style: Golden Hour Stickman (user's own style)

## Three candidate structures
1. **Straight two-hander, one location.** Hallway outside the classroom, both sat by the door. Three lines, a held silence, both turn to camera, black. Pure sitcom timing; the camera tightens as the panic grows.
2. **Expectation / reality split.** Top half: A studying all night; bottom half: B doing the assignment — then both meet at the door. Clever, but it spends the 15 s explaining and spoils the reveal.
3. **Cold open on the faces.** Open on the final double stare, rewind "6 hours earlier"… Too much framing for a 3-line joke; kills the punch.

**Chosen: 1.** The joke is a double reveal — each one is calm about the thing the other one forgot. It needs zero setup and lands hardest when nothing gets in the way. Every second goes to timing and faces.

## Logline and arc
Two friends chill at the classroom door — until they find out, one line at a time, that there's an exam *and* an assignment and each of them only knew about one.
- Setup (0–3 s): golden morning, relaxed, A asks casually.
- Turn (3–8 s): B is smugly unbothered ("What exam?"), A is blank ("What assignment?").
- Ending (8–15 s): silence → both turn slowly to camera, faces drain, Lacrimosa slams in, hard cut to black, school bell rings on black.

## Benchmark
- Learn: mockumentary sitcom "look to camera" holds; the internet "Lacrimosa realisation" edit (choir as instant doom); anime magic-hour school hallways (window light as mood).
- Don't take: any characters, layouts, shots or the meme's specific footage. Music is Mozart's composition (public domain) in a free recording or our own rendition.

## Revision 2 (user notes): skeletons
After "What assignment?" they look at each other, then at the camera. On the Lacrimosa hit (10.19) they flicker x-ray style
(dark body + bones, every other 2 frames) into skeletons (10.59) built on the rig's own joints, so the pose is identical.
The terrified skeletons stare (sockets with trembling pupils), rattle (12.64), then crumble (12.94): 17 rigid pieces each,
analytic fall → one bounce → rest; skulls pop up and land last (≈13.6) and roll apart. Camera pulls back to show the pile. Black at 14.40.
Sound: x-ray zap gated at 15 Hz, chattering rattle, one bone clack per landing (times exported from the picture via events.json), skull bonks.
