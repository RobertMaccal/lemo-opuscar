# THE 10-SECOND MASSAGE — treatment

Style: Golden Hour Stickman (the user's own style, `my-styles/golden-hour-stickman/STYLE.md`).
1080×1920, 30 fps, ~51 s, English dialogue (two ElevenLabs voices), no narrator, Chopin Nocturne Op. 9 No. 2 (Musopen CC0 recording).

## 1. Three candidate structures

**A. One evening, told straight (chosen).** Montage of her giving → the request → the bird → the catch → ten real seconds → the flex → her stare. The story is a contrast, so it needs the long "giving" first and the short "giving back" second, in the same couch, from the same camera. The user's beat list is already this shape; the work is in timing and acting.

**B. Expectation / reality split-screen.** Top half: what he thinks his massage looks like (heroic, slow, glowing); bottom half: what it is. Funny, but it explains the joke instead of letting her face land it, and a vertical split halves every face.

**C. The stopwatch.** A kitchen timer on the coffee table, wound by her during the montage (minutes), and by him for his turn (ten seconds), ding. Clear, but a visible counter turns the ending into a number gag and gives away the beat before her face can.

Chose **A**: it keeps the punchline on a face, which is what this style is for. From B we keep one idea: the music is far more beautiful than the massage. From C we keep one prop, quietly: a wall calendar whose page turns at each montage match cut (MON → TUE → WED → THU) — the only text inside the story world.

## 2. Logline and arc

*A husband who melts under his wife's daily massages tries to dodge giving one back with the oldest trick in the book, gets caught by the sleeve, and delivers exactly ten seconds of effort with the pride of a champion.*

- **Setup** (0–10 s): every day, she gives; every day, he melts a little further into the couch.
- **Turn** (10–32 s): "Can you give me one now?" — the bird, the sneak, the sleeve, "I was joking!"
- **Ending** (32–51 s): ten honest seconds, hands off, a flex, "You cool?" — and her face. Cut to black on her stare. The ending echoes the opening shot exactly with the roles swapped (same couch, same framing, she sits where he sat) — the callback is the joke.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Learn from:** the held reaction shot of mockumentary sitcoms (the joke is the silence after the line); classic cartoon acting for anticipation in the sneak (lean, rise, tiptoe, look back); magic-hour anime interiors for the window light that changes day by day.
- **Do not take:** any characters, layouts, gags, music cues, typefaces or shots from them. Nothing from the style's demo film (school, exams, races, a friend): this is a two-hander in one living room.

## 4. Geography (fixed for the whole film)

Front view of one living room, wall-flat. Back wall: **tall window screen-left** (sun comes from the left, low, golden; every rim light is on the left of the figures, cast shadows fall to the right), **door to the hallway screen-right**. The **couch** stands in the middle, pulled a little away from the back wall so a person can stand behind it; it never moves. A **plant** by the window, a **small round coffee table** front-right, a **soft rug** under it all, a lamp and a framed picture over the couch, a wall calendar by the door.

- The masseur always stands **behind the couch**, facing the camera, behind the seated person's back. Both faces are visible at once — the receiver in front, the giver above and behind. Nobody ever faces each other during a massage.
- In the montage he sits centre, she stands behind. In his turn, she sits centre, he stands behind: the same shot, mirrored roles.
- The door is reached by walking right, in front of the couch's right arm. The way behind the couch is around its right end.

## 5. Cast

- **HER (the wife)** — soft violet top `#8E6FC4`, dark trousers, ink hair with a ponytail. Patient, observant, playfully firm. Her brows are the punchline.
- **HIM (the husband)** — deep-blue hoodie `#2F4F9E` with cuffs (the sleeve she catches), dark trousers, short messy ink hair. Lazy, lovable, shameless, convinced he is great.
- Both: indigo ink limbs, cream heads, rim light from the window.

## 12. Not the demo again (DIRECTOR.md §11)

Compared with the style's demo, *That One Friend at School*: structure (one evening, a contrast in two halves vs. a school day of escalating defeats) — different; opening (inside the scene mid-massage vs. a corridor title) — different; signature shot (ten uninterrupted seconds of a locked two-shot vs. eyes-then-head / dolly-zoom) — different; camera path (one fixed room, front-on, cuts and pushes vs. many locations, crane-up) — different; score shape (theme → suspended → the ornamented statement under the massage → cadenza cut dead vs. the demo's sting/stretch/montage edit) — partly shared (same nocturne, by the brief's request); ending (a held reaction and a cut to black vs. a magic-hour crane up) — different. Five of six differ.
