# THAT ONE FRIEND AT SCHOOL — treatment

Vertical 1080×1920, 30 fps, about 2:25 (2:15–2:40 once the dialogue is recorded). English. Dialogue only, no narrator.
Style: the user's own brief — a cinematic, nostalgic 2D stickman world, drawn and animated entirely in code (Canvas 2D, one `render(t)` page, captured frame by frame, mixed with ffmpeg).

## 1. Structure: three candidates, one choice

The screenplay's five sequences and final conversation are fixed. What is ours to decide is how they are shaped.

- **A. One school day that keeps getting worse (chosen).** Straight chronology, but the *light* tells the time: nervous morning corridor → hard backlit exam hall → drained lavender spiral → amber afternoon class → loud noon montage → sunset. Each sequence is shot one size tighter or one notch faster than the last, until the JACK wall, then the film exhales.
- **B. A memory told from the sunset.** Open on the courtyard, flash back. Rejected: it gives away the calm ending and spends the first three seconds without a joke.
- **C. A running scoreboard.** A tally card after every sequence. Rejected: it turns the repetition gag (which belongs to sequence five) into the whole film and makes Jack look like a rival, not a friend.

Why A: the comedy needs the audience to believe Jack in sequence one, and A is the only shape that lets every reveal land when YOU get it. The bookend is light, not plot: the same window light that opens the corridor closes the film, low and gold.

## 2. Logline and arc

An ordinary student spends one school day discovering that his relaxed best friend, who "didn't study either", is better than him at everything and has no idea.

- Setup: "Same here." YOU relax. We believe Jack.
- Turn: a hand goes up. "Could I have another answer booklet, please?"
- Escalation: the humerus, the hyoid, second place, second place, second place.
- Ending: "I'm just chill." YOU give up, fondly. Two friends, long shadows, Chopin.

## 3. Benchmark

- **Learn:** the held reaction shot of mockumentary sitcoms (cut to the face, hold one beat longer than is comfortable); montage built on a repeated sound hit that arrives sooner each time; the dolly-zoom as a picture of dread; anime "magic hour" light, where the window patch on the floor is a character.
- **Don't take:** anyone's characters, designs, shots, music cues or lettering. Everything on screen is drawn here.

## 6. Cue map

- **Chopin, Nocturne in E-flat major, Op. 9 No. 2** (12/8, Andante, about 60 dotted-crotchets per minute) is the spine.
  A newly rendered performance from the public-domain score on a sampled grand, or a recording whose licence we have verified (the Wikimedia file named in the brief gets checked first). No commercial recording.
- Opening: bars 1–4, full tone. Exam: the same phrase at −18 dB under the clock.
- Booklet line: hard cut mid-phrase on the word "please", then one low piano cluster as the sting.
- Spiral: the theme slowed and low-passed, a semitone clash added, heartbeat at 54 → 96 bpm.
- Classroom: bars 5–8, light. Stops dead on "The hyoid bone."
- Montage: the nocturne's melody re-barred as a quick 2/4 "galop" (same notes, staccato, left-hand oom-pah, snare brush, woodblock on every board flip); tempo steps 112 → 132 → 152 → 176.
- Sunset: the closing bars and the final E-flat chord, slower, room reverb, birds over it.
- Picture locks to these cues: every board flip, the pencil drop, the desk thud and each cut in the montage sit on a beat.

- Title: Lilita One, cream with a deep-indigo edge, "FRIEND" in sunlight yellow, tilted 2°, on screen inside the first second.
- Captions: a paper label from the school's own stationery, with the speaker's colour tab (YOU blue, JACK green). Emphasis word in rust. YOUR thoughts get a dashed lavender label. Selective: only the lines that carry a joke.

## 9. Voices (after visual approval; nothing generated yet)

ElevenLabs, real voice IDs from the connected account: YOU (young, expressive), JACK (lower, soft, deadpan), PROFESSOR, TEACHER, COACH, STUDENT A, STUDENT B. YOUR thoughts use the same voice, closer and drier. One take per line, mouths driven by each clip's loudness and timing.

## 10. As built

- Running time 2:37.7 at 30 fps, 1080×1920, H.264 + AAC, −14 LUFS. 45 dialogue lines (ElevenLabs, Eleven v4), every line checked by speech-to-text on the takes and again on the final mix.
- Two lines were re-taken for clarity ("Second?" and "Yeah. After Jack."). One short line was added to the script: the professor's "Alright, everyone. In you come."
- Music: the CC0 Musopen recording of the nocturne throughout (opening, classroom, ending as played; time-stretched 1.55× → 2.5× with added percussion for the montage; slowed and low-passed for the spiral). The MIDI edition considered for a re-performance is CC BY-SA, so it was not used.
- Staging changes from the storyboard: in the corridor the door now sits between Jack and YOU, so the professor can open it between them; the pool-side deck chair became a bench.
- Not built from a library style or its demo: the look follows the brief, the engine is this folder's `src/`.
